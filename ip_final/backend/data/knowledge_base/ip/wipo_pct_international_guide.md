# WIPO International IP Systems: PCT, Madrid and Hague

The World Intellectual Property Organization (WIPO) administers treaties that streamline cross-border protection for patents, trademarks, and industrial designs across over 150 contracting member states.

## 1. Patent Cooperation Treaty (PCT) — International Patents
The PCT enables applicants to seek patent protection for an invention simultaneously in over 155 countries by filing a single "international" patent application.

### Key Milestones & Timelines
- **Month 0 (Priority Date)**: File first national patent application (e.g., in the Indian Patent Office).
- **Month 12 (Paris Convention Deadline)**: File International PCT Application before the 12-month convention priority period expires.
- **Month 16 (International Search Report / ISR & WO-ISA)**: An International Searching Authority (such as ISA/IN, ISA/EP, ISA/US) produces an ISR establishing global prior art citations and an initial Written Opinion on novelty, inventive step, and industrial applicability.
- **Month 18 (International Publication)**: The PCT application is published globally on WIPO PATENTSCOPE.
- **Month 30 / 31 (National Phase Entry)**: The applicant enters the "National Phase" in individual target jurisdictions (e.g., USPTO, EPO, JPO, CNIPA, UKIPO). This extends the decision window and fee commitments by up to 18 months compared to the direct Paris Convention route.

## 2. Madrid System — International Trademarks
- Administered by WIPO under the Madrid Protocol.
- Allows an Indian trademark owner with a basic application or registration in India to seek trademark protection in up to 130 member countries through a single international application filed via IP India.

## 3. Hague System — International Industrial Designs
- Facilitates the acquisition of design protection in multiple contracting countries through a centralized filing with WIPO.
