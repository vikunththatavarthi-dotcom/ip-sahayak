# Patentability and Filing in India

Indian patent applications are assessed under the Patents Act, 1970 and Patents Rules, 2003. In broad terms, an invention must satisfy the statutory definition of invention and should be assessed for novelty, inventive step and industrial applicability, while also considering exclusions under section 3 and section 4.

Prior-art searching should take place before public disclosure and should include relevant patent literature and non-patent literature. A search reduces uncertainty but is not a guarantee of grant, validity, freedom to operate, or non-infringement. The Patent Office examines the application and may issue objections that require a response within the applicable process and time limits.

Filing, examination, pre-grant opposition, post-grant opposition, infringement and licensing are distinct legal topics. Current forms, official fees, deadlines and procedural requirements should be confirmed directly through IP India or a registered patent agent because they can change.

For Ayurveda or traditional-knowledge-related inventions, also retrieve the traditional-knowledge material and assess prior-art and section 3(p) implications. This is informational guidance rather than legal advice or a patentability opinion.
