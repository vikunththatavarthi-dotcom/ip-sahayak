# Geographical Indications Protection in India

The Geographical Indications of Goods (Registration and Protection) Act, 1999 governs the registration and protection of Geographical Indications (GIs) in India. A Geographical Indication identifies goods as originating in a specific territory, region, or locality where a given quality, reputation, or other characteristic is essentially attributable to its geographical origin.

## Eligible Goods Categories
GIs apply to:
1. **Agricultural Goods**: e.g., Darjeeling Tea, Basmati Rice, Malabar Pepper, Alphonso Mango.
2. **Natural Goods**: e.g., Makrana Marble.
3. **Manufactured & Handicraft Goods**: e.g., Kanchipuram Silk, Pashmina Shawls, Mysore Sandalwood Oil, Firozabad Glass.
4. **Foodstuffs**: e.g., Tirupati Laddu, Hyderabad Haleem, Bikaneri Bhujia.

## Key Statutory Rules & Differences from Trademarks
- **Collective/Community Right**: A GI belongs to the producers and community of the specified territory. It cannot be granted to a single private commercial enterprise.
- **Prohibition on Assignment (Section 24)**: A registered Geographical Indication cannot be assigned, licensed, transmitted, or mortgaged. Any such agreement is void under Indian law.
- **Authorized User Status (Section 17)**: Any producer or manufacturer operating in the designated geographic territory who produces goods according to standard historical methods may apply as an "Authorized User" to utilize the registered GI logo and certification.
- **Duration**: GI registration is valid for 10 years and is renewable indefinitely upon payment of renewal fees.

## Infringement & Protection
Unauthorized use of a registered GI or deceptive indication of origin constitutes infringement and unfair competition under Section 22. GIs help protect traditional community knowledge, indigenous craft techniques, and agricultural heritage against deceptive commercial exploitation.
