# Cross-Border Patent Prosecution: Section 39, USPTO and EPO

When expanding patent protection from India to foreign jurisdictions such as the United States (USPTO) or Europe (EPO), applicants must comply with Indian statutory prerequisites and international examination standards.

## 1. Indian Patents Act Section 39 — Mandatory Foreign Filing License (FFL)
- Under Section 39 of the Patents Act, 1970, any person **resident in India** who wishes to file a patent application outside India must:
  1. File the initial patent application in India and wait at least **6 weeks** without receiving any secrecy direction under Section 35; OR
  2. Obtain prior written permission / Foreign Filing License from the Controller of Patents via **Form 25**.
- **Penalty for Non-Compliance (Section 40 & 118)**: Filing abroad without Section 39 clearance results in the abandonment of the Indian patent application, revocation of any granted patent, and criminal penalties (imprisonment up to 2 years and/or fines).

## 2. United States Patent and Trademark Office (USPTO) Standards
- **Statutory Requirements**: 35 U.S.C. § 101 (Eligible Subject Matter), § 102 (Novelty & Prior Art), § 103 (Non-Obviousness).
- **Duty of Candor & Disclosure (Rule 56)**: Applicants and their attorneys have an absolute legal duty to disclose all known prior art, foreign examination search reports (including TKDL or Indian IPO office actions), by filing an Information Disclosure Statement (IDS).
- **Patent Eligibility (Alice/Mayo Framework)**: Natural extracts and diagnostic correlations require specific non-routine technical applications.

## 3. European Patent Office (EPO) Standards
- **Statutory Requirements**: Articles 52-57 EPC (Novelty, Inventive Step, Industrial Applicability).
- **Problem-Solution Approach**: The EPO strictly assesses inventive step by identifying the closest prior art, defining the objective technical problem, and determining whether the claimed solution is obvious to the skilled person.
- **Strict Added-Matter Rule (Article 123(2) EPC)**: European practice strictly prohibits introducing amendments that extend beyond the content of the initial application as filed.
