# Traditional Knowledge, TKDL and Section 3(p) of the Patents Act

Section 3(p) of the Patents Act, 1970 excludes "an invention which in effect, is traditional knowledge or which is an aggregation or duplication of known properties of traditionally known component or components." This is distinct from the exclusions in sections 3(c), 3(d), and 3(e); the applicable objection depends on the actual claim and evidence.

The Traditional Knowledge Digital Library (TKDL) documents codified Indian traditional knowledge, including Ayurveda, Siddha, Unani and Sowa Rigpa material, so that it can be considered during patent examination as prior art. It is a prior-art and anti-misappropriation resource, not a substitute for a patent search or a grant decision.

For an Ayurveda-related innovation, a filing strategy should separate what is already disclosed in classical or other prior art from the proposed technical contribution. A claim may need support for novelty, inventive step and industrial applicability under the Patents Act, as well as a careful review of sections 3(p), 3(d), 3(e) and other exclusions where relevant. A qualified patent professional should review the claims and evidence before filing.

This material is for IP and traditional-knowledge context only. It does not establish a medical use, safety, efficacy, dosage, or regulatory approval of any herb or formulation.
